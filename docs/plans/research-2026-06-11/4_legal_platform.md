# Legal & Platform-Policy Constraints on Real-Time AI Listening/Coaching Tools (as of 2026-06-11)

## 1. US Recording-Consent Law + AI-Specific Litigation

**All-party consent states (current list):** 11 strict all-party states — **California, Delaware, Florida, Illinois, Maryland, Massachusetts, Montana, Nevada, New Hampshire, Pennsylvania, Washington** — plus **Connecticut**, which is one-party criminally but all-party for civil liability, so treat as a 12th ([Justia 50-state survey](https://www.justia.com/50-state-surveys/recording-phone-calls-and-conversations/); [RecordingLaw 2026 guide](https://www.recordinglaw.com/party-two-party-consent-states/)). Federal law (18 U.S.C. §2511) is one-party consent. Maryland was considering loosening its rules in late 2025 but no change has landed ([WYPR](https://www.wypr.org/wypr-news/2025-11-20/maryland-considers-altering-its-recording-consent-and-evidence-laws)).

**Does real-time STT without saved audio count as "recording"?** Courts and plaintiffs' bars are treating it as covered. Three theories matter:

1. **Even "transitory" capture is a recording.** Reed Smith's analysis: a recording is "most likely" made by the software even if transitory, and the law focuses on the act of capturing a private conversation, not the storage technology ([Reed Smith](https://www.reedsmith.com/our-insights/blogs/employment-law-watch/102ls2n/the-legality-of-ai-powered-recording-and-transcription/)). California's CIPA §631 separately prohibits **reading/learning contents in transit** — interception, not just recording — so "we never store audio" is not a defense.
2. **The AI vendor as third-party eavesdropper.** *Ambriz v. Google* (N.D. Cal., MTD denied **Feb 10, 2025**): Google's Contact Center AI, which provides real-time transcripts and live agent suggestions, can be a CIPA wiretapper if it merely has the **"capability"** to use the data for its own benefit — actual use need not be shown ([Goodwin](https://www.goodwinlaw.com/en/insights/publications/2025/02/alerts-practices-dpc-ftec-ai-voice-products-subject-to-california-invasion-of-privacy-claims); [Paul Hastings](https://www.paulhastings.com/insights/ph-privacy/ai-transcription-tools-when-a-robot-is-listening-courts-may-find-it-is-wiretapping)).
3. **Generic disclosures are insufficient.** "This call may be monitored" notices are being tested when third-party AI is on the line ([Fisher Phillips](https://www.fisherphillips.com/en/news-insights/ai-call-monitoring-lawsuits-are-heating-up.html)).

**Key 2025–2026 lawsuits:**
- ***In re Otter.AI Privacy Litigation*** (N.D. Cal.): lead case *Brewer v. Otter.ai* filed Aug 15, 2025; four class suits consolidated Oct 22, 2025; consolidated complaint Dec 5, 2025; claims under ECPA, CFAA, CIPA, common-law privacy, UCL. Core theory: Otter obtained consent only from the host (sometimes not even that), not all participants, and used recordings for model training. MTD hearing was scheduled **May 20, 2026**; case ongoing ([NatLawReview/Jackson Lewis](https://natlawreview.com/article/ai-notetaking-tools-under-fire-lessons-otterai-class-action-complaint); [Michalsons](https://www.michalsons.com/blog/justin-brewer-v-otter-ai-consent-for-ai-meeting-assistants/79576); [Fisher Phillips](https://www.fisherphillips.com/en/news-insights/new-lawsuit-highlights-concerns-about-ai-notetakers.html)).
- ***Galanter v. Cresta*** (filed Jun 13, 2025): real-time conversation-intelligence/agent-coaching software as CIPA wiretap — the closest analogue to a live sales-coaching product ([Debevoise](https://www.debevoisedatablog.com/2025/06/04/cipa-litigation-trends-regarding-tracking-technology-and-ai/); [FKKS](https://technologylaw.fkks.com/post/102kz0c/ai-recording-notetaking-tools-trigger-wave-of-lawsuits-could-your-business-be)).
- ***ConverseNow*** (N.D. Cal., MTD denied Aug 11, 2025): AI voice agent for restaurant calls; CIPA claims proceed ([Wilson Sonsini](https://www.wsgr.com/en/insights/us-federal-court-allows-cipa-class-action-against-ai-customer-service-provider-to-proceed.html)).
- ***Heartland Dental / RingCentral*** (N.D. Ill., filed Jul 3, 2025): wiretap claims over RingCentral AI on customer calls ([Fisher Phillips](https://www.fisherphillips.com/en/insights/insights/new-class-action-targets-healthcare-ai-recordings)).

**Legislative relief stalled:** CIPA reform bill **SB 690** (a "commercial business purpose" exception) passed the CA Senate unanimously Jun 3, 2025, but was held in the Assembly as a two-year bill; CIPA exposure persists through at least 2026 ([Duane Morris](https://www.duanemorris.com/alerts/california_sb690_stalls_assembly_cipa_liability_remains_least_through_2026_0725.html)).

**Planning implication:** A coaching tool that streams the *other party's* audio to a cloud STT/LLM is, on current case law, performing an interception requiring all-party consent in the 12 states above — and the vendor itself (not just the user) can be sued under the *Ambriz* "capability" theory.

## 2. EU: GDPR + AI Act

**GDPR.** Call/meeting transcription is personal-data processing requiring a lawful basis: typically **consent** (affirmative, granular — recording consent ≠ consent to AI-training reuse) or **legitimate interest** with a documented balancing test, defined retention, and transparency to all participants ([heyData](https://heydata.eu/en/magazine/balancing-trust-and-control-how-to-make-ai-recorded-online-meetings-gdpr-compliant/); [GDPR Advisor](https://www.gdpr-advisor.com/gdpr-compliance-for-voice-to-text-services-and-transcription-platforms/)). Voice can be biometric data; secret transcription in Germany also risks **§201 StGB** (criminal recording of the spoken word), and consent must be informed and explicit ([Lutz Abel](https://www.lutzabel.com/en/article/ai-transcription-of-meetings-data-protection-section-201-of-the-german-criminal-code-stgb-and-the-limits-of-consent/)).

**EU AI Act — emotion recognition.** Article 5(1)(f) **prohibits** (since **Feb 2, 2025**) AI inferring emotions of natural persons **in the workplace** from biometric data (incl. voice), with medical/safety exceptions; fines up to **€35M or 7% of global turnover** ([FPF](https://fpf.org/blog/red-lines-under-eu-ai-act-unpacking-the-prohibition-of-emotion-recognition-in-the-workplace-and-education-institutions/); [Art. 5 text](https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/article/5/); [DLA Piper](https://www.technologyslegaledge.com/2025/04/eu-ai-act-spotlight-on-emotional-recognition-systems-in-the-workplace/)). Critical nuances for a coaching product: analyzing the **customer's** emotions is not within the workplace prohibition (but may be high-risk/Annex III); analyzing the **employee-user's** emotions is prohibited; the Commission's Feb 2025 guidelines suggest a narrow "training purposes" carve-out if results never reach HR or affect employment — but that carve-out has no textual basis in the Act itself, so relying on it is risky ([Wolters Kluwer](https://legalblogs.wolterskluwer.com/global-workplace-law-and-policy/the-prohibition-of-ai-emotion-recognition-technologies-in-the-workplace-under-the-ai-act/)). Sentiment-from-text/word-content (not voice tone/biometrics) falls outside the biometric-based prohibition.

**Works councils.** In Germany, BetrVG §87(1)(6) gives works councils mandatory codetermination over any technology *objectively capable* of monitoring employee performance — AI transcription/coaching tools qualify even absent monitoring intent; rollout without works-council agreement can be enjoined ([Orrick](https://www.orrick.com/en/Insights/2024/09/AI-and-German-Co-Determination-What-Employers-Need-to-Know); [Heuking](https://www.heuking.de/en/news-events/newsletter-articles/detail/legally-compliant-use-of-ai-transcription-tools-in-businesses.html)).

Third-party bots join as pseudo-participants; hosts can eject; no marketplace path equivalent to Zoom's for raw media. | Host admission controls; Workspace admin restrictions. No documented mass ban of desktop-capture apps found. |
| **Local/loopback capture (all platforms)** | Capturing system audio on your own device (Granola model) bypasses platform bot controls entirely; none of the three platforms' public TOS were found to expressly prohibit user-side OS-level capture — platform terms push recording-law compliance onto the host/user ([Zoom ToS](https://www.zoom.com/en/trust/terms/); [Granola docs](https://docs.granola.ai/help-center/consent-security-privacy/security-privacy-data-faqs)). | No enforcement evidence found against loopback-capture apps by platforms (they are invisible to the platform). The 2026 bot-blocking wave does **not** reach them. |

## 4. Distribution / OS Constraints (Electron, system audio + screen)

- **macOS:** ScreenCaptureKit (macOS 13+) is the supported API for system audio + screen; audio-only via Core Audio process taps (macOS 14.2+) ([Apple docs](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/CoreAudio/capturing-system-audio-with-core-audio-taps)). TCC permission required; **Sequoia re-prompts users monthly** to re-confirm screen/system-audio capture (was weekly in betas), plus an "Allow for one session" option — a recurring UX tax on capture apps ([MacRumors](https://www.macrumors.com/2024/10/07/apple-screen-recording-popup-update/); [Apple Support](https://support.apple.com/guide/mac-help/control-access-screen-system-audio-recording-mchld6aa7d23/mac)). Sandboxed Mac App Store distribution of system-audio capture is impractical; ship Developer ID + **notarization** instead ([Electron code-signing docs](https://www.electronjs.org/docs/latest/tutorial/code-signing)). Note: cert changes can orphan previously granted TCC permissions ([Apple dev forums via search](https://developer.apple.com/forums/tags/screencapturekit?page=2)).
- **Windows:** WASAPI loopback capture of render devices needs **no consent prompt** (only microphone activation triggers one), though global mic privacy settings can still interfere ([Microsoft ActivateAudioInterfaceAsync docs](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/win32/api/mmdeviceapi/nf-mmdeviceapi-activateaudiointerfaceasync); [Loopback recording docs](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/win32/coreaudio/loopback-recording)). Main risk is reputational: unsigned/OV-signed Electron apps hit SmartScreen warnings and Defender false positives (a 2022 Defender update flagged Electron apps broadly: Discord, Slack, VS Code); EV certs or Azure Trusted/Artifact Signing mitigate; each update's new hash resets reputation ([Bitdefender](https://www.bitdefender.com/en-us/blog/hotforsecurity/microsoft-fixes-chromium-electron-false-positive-flagging); [electron-builder #5759](https://github.com/electron-userland/electron-builder/issues/5759)). EDR in enterprises commonly flags screen+audio capture combined with network exfiltration patterns — expect corporate-IT blocking similar to UCR's bot ban.

## 5. How Shipping Products Handle Consent

| Product | Mechanism | Liability allocation |
|---|---|---|
| **Otter** | Recording starts on user/host action; bot announces itself in meetings (post-lawsuit emphasis) | ToS: user represents/warrants they have "all rights, licenses, and permissions"; consent responsibility on account holder ([Otter ToS](https://otter.ai/terms-of-service)); plaintiffs argue auto-join design defeats this ([Steno Imperium](https://stenoimperium.com/2025/08/20/the-otter-lawsuit-ai-privacy-and-the-fight-over-consent/)) |
| **Fireflies** | Opt-in/opt-out meeting controls, bot visible in participant list | ToS: user "must ensure that each meeting participant consents"; broad indemnification of Fireflies by user ([Fireflies ToS](https://fireflies.ai/terms-of-service); [consent controls](https://guide.fireflies.ai/articles/7003995379-Recording+Consent+and+Meeting+Compliance+in+Fireflies:+Opt-In,+Opt-Out,+and+Controls)) |
| **Granola** | No bot; local capture; **off-by-default** "Let others know you're using Granola" auto-message (Labs setting); docs "strongly recommend" informing participants | Consent obligation entirely on user; Granola states "you are responsible for obtaining consent" ([Granola FAQs](https://docs.granola.ai/help-center/consent-security-privacy/security-privacy-data-faqs)) |
| **Cluely** | Marketed "undetectable" overlay; **no built-in disclosure mechanism** | ToS/privacy policy require user to inform participants and obtain consent — directly contradicting the marketing; all liability on user ([Cluely Terms](https://cluely.com/terms); [Greenberg Glusker](https://www.greenbergglusker.com/news/privacy-risks-abound-with-cheat-on-everything-ai-apps)) |

## 6. Granola-Style "No Bot, Local Capture" — Legal Analyses

The consistent legal conclusion: **invisible architecture changes nothing legally.** Wiretap/eavesdropping statutes turn on capturing the communication, not on whether a bot is visible; in all-party states and under GDPR the user must still inform and obtain consent before a Granola session, and the silent design arguably *increases* risk because it removes the implicit notice a visible bot provides ([Zack Proser compliance analysis](https://zackproser.com/blog/granola-call-recording-legal-compliance); [Granola's own docs](https://docs.granola.ai/help-center/consent-security-privacy/security-privacy-data-faqs)). The Otter complaint's theory that products "designed" to capture without all-party consent are defective regardless of ToS disclaimers ([NatLawReview](https://natlawreview.com/article/ai-notetaking-tools-under-fire-lessons-otterai-class-action-complaint)), combined with *Ambriz*'s vendor-as-eavesdropper "capability" test, means a no-bot vendor is **not** insulated from direct CIPA/ECPA liability — especially if it trains models on transcripts. No court has yet ruled specifically on a no-bot local-capture product; that is an open question.

**Bottom line for compliance planning:** build all-party consent into the product flow (default-on disclosure, geo-aware blocking in the 12 US states absent confirmed consent), avoid voice-based emotion inference about the user in EU workplaces, never train on customer conversations without separate consent, and don't rely on ToS liability-shifting — *Otter* shows courts may treat "consent is the user's problem" plus auto-capture design as the defect itself.
