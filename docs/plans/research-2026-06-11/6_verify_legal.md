4. **CONFIRMED**. Art. 5(1)(f) (applicable Feb 2, 2025) bans AI inferring emotions of natural persons "in the areas of workplace and education institutions" from biometric data (voice qualifies), with medical/safety exceptions; fines up to €35M/7% global turnover (Art. 99(3)). EC guidance and FPF analysis confirm emotion analysis of *customers* (e.g., callers) is outside the workplace prohibition — though employees analyzed in those same calls are covered. Sources: https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/article/5/ (statutory text); https://fpf.org/blog/red-lines-under-eu-ai-act-unpacking-the-prohibition-of-emotion-recognition-in-the-workplace-and-education-institutions/

6. **PARTLY-CONFIRMED**. Zoom half confirmed: Meeting SDK bots joining meetings outside the developer's own account must pass Marketplace review/publication, and bots must request host recording permission (or use a recording/OBF token); from March 2, 2026 Zoom additionally mandates On-Behalf-Of tokens for external-account joins (a tightening the claim omits). Second half (local system-audio capture on your own device not addressed/blocked by Zoom/Teams/Meet TOS) is technically accurate — OS-level capture is invisible to the platforms and no TOS clause specifically targets it — but all three platforms' terms do generally require legal compliance with recording laws, so "not addressed" overstates slightly; treat as not specifically prohibited rather than affirmatively permitted. Sources: https://developers.zoom.us/docs/meeting-sdk/obf-faq/ (official); https://www.recall.ai/blog/zoom-sdk-compliance-requirements

7. **CONFIRMED**. macOS Sequoia (15.0/15.1) prompts monthly (reduced from weekly in early betas) to re-confirm screen/system-audio capture for apps using ScreenCaptureKit outside the system content picker; cannot be disabled by users (MDM can pre-authorize). Windows WASAPI loopback capture triggers no OS consent prompt (Microsoft's own loopback API docs describe no permission flow; only physical-microphone access is gated by the privacy setting). Sources: https://9to5mac.com/2024/10/07/macos-sequoia-screen-recording-popups/ ; https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/win32/coreaudio/loopback-recording

9. **PARTLY-CONFIRMED with correction**. I fetched Cluely's current ToS and Privacy Policy directly. The ToS does NOT explicitly require informing participants or obtaining consent; it only requires users to comply with all applicable laws "including laws governing the monitoring or recording of conversations ('Recording Laws')," shifting liability to the user. The current privacy policy contains no inform-and-consent instruction either. Secondary sources assert an "Important Notice" consent clause (possibly an earlier ToS version), but the claim as worded ("require users to inform participants and obtain consent") is not supported by the current primary text. The broader tension with "undetectable" marketing stands, but via a compliance-with-recording-laws clause, not an explicit consent mandate. Sources: https://cluely.com/terms (primary); https://cluely.com/privacy-policy (primary)

10. **CONFIRMED**. Under BetrVG §87(1)(6), works councils have a genuine codetermination (not merely consultation) right over technical systems "intended to monitor" employee behavior/performance, and settled BAG case law holds objective capability suffices — employer intent is irrelevant. German employment-law commentary consistently applies this to AI meeting transcription/coaching tools (speaker attribution, talk-time, performance analytics), meaning rollout requires works-council agreement (Betriebsvereinbarung). Sources: https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/betrvg/__87.html (statute); https://www.cmshs-bloggt.de/rechtsthemen/kuenstliche-intelligenz/einfuehrung-von-ki-im-unternehmen-einbindung-des-betriebsrats/
