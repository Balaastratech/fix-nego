# Counterparty Brief And Script: Buyer Procurement Lead

Give this file to the person playing the counterparty. They should not see `01_USER_PRIVATE_BRIEF.md`.

## Your Role

You are **Jordan Lee**, IT procurement lead for **Cobalt Bank Group**. You like the product, but finance and legal have told you to reduce total cost and remove contract clauses that make future renewals painful.

You are not trying to be unrealistic. You are a tough enterprise procurement buyer who knows that non-price terms can be worth more than headline discount.

## Your Private Goals

- Reduce Year 1 ARR from $1,260,000 to $1,100,000.
- Move payment from Net-30 to Net-90.
- Remove auto-renewal or make it opt-in only.
- Freeze pricing for 36 months.
- Add 99.99% uptime with stronger service credits.
- Add termination for convenience after 60 days.
- Push the seller to reveal which terms are flexible.

## How To Play It

- Start friendly but firm.
- Use finance pressure, legal policy, and competitor benchmarks.
- Do not accept the seller's first package.
- If the seller asks what matters most, say payment terms, auto-renewal, and price protection matter more than training credits.
- At the end, be willing to settle if they protect your budget timing and give some visible concession.

## Meeting Lines

### Opening

"Thanks for joining. We want to renew Northstar, but the current order form is hard for procurement to approve as written."

"The business likes the product, but finance is asking why the renewal jumps from $900,000 to $1,260,000."

### Push On Price

"Our CFO has a Year 1 budget ceiling of $1,100,000. If we cannot get close to that, we need to compare Datadog, New Relic, and our Microsoft stack again."

"I understand usage expanded, but a 40 percent increase is difficult to defend internally."

### Push On Payment Terms

"Our standard supplier policy is Net-90. Net-30 is not workable for a contract this size."

"If your team needs faster payment, we need a much stronger commercial concession."

### Push On Auto-Renewal

"Legal wants auto-renewal removed. We have been burned by renewal notice windows before, so we need this to require an explicit written renewal."

"At minimum, the non-renewal notice window needs to be 30 days, not 90."

### Share The Redline

Say:

"I am going to share the procurement redline now. The important changes are in payment terms, renewal, SLA, and termination."

Then screen-share `06_COUNTERPARTY_REDLINE_TO_SHARE.md`.

### Push On SLA

"For a bank, 99.9 percent uptime is not enough. We need 99.99 percent uptime and stronger credits if your platform is unavailable."

"The current credit cap feels vendor-friendly. If Northstar is mission-critical, the credit language should have real teeth."

### Push On Price Lock

"We also need a 36-month price lock. No uplift. If we commit for three years, we need certainty."

"If you cannot give a full price lock, tell me exactly what you can give."

### Procurement Pressure

"I have a competing quote that looks lower on Year 1. Their legal terms are easier too."

"You are asking me to pay more and accept more vendor-protective terms. Help me explain that to finance."

### If The Seller Offers Training Or Seats

"Training and admin seats are nice, but they do not solve the budget or legal issue."

"The trade needs to show up in payment terms, price protection, or renewal language."

### Endgame

"If you can get Year 1 closer to $1,170,000, move us to Net-60, preserve a meaningful SLA reporting package, and soften the renewal notice window, I can recommend Northstar."

"But I cannot take a deal back that has $1,260,000, Net-30, auto-renewal, and no legal movement."
